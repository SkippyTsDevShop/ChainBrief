What's Actually Driving This Month's Layer-2 and DeFi Rally

Starknet up 21% in a day. Uniswap up 25%. It's tempting to credit any one headline — but this rally is the product of at least four separate stories landing in the same two weeks, and they don't all deserve equal credit.

By ChainBrief Staff ·  September 21, 2026 ·  6 min read
Share on X Share on LinkedIn

Layer-2 tokens — the assets tied to scaling networks like Starknet, Arbitrum, and Optimism that process transactions off Ethereum's main chain but settle back to it — and DeFi tokens broadly posted some of their sharpest gains of the year this week. Starknet (STRK) rose as much as 21% over 24 hours, Arbitrum (ARB) 25%, and Uniswap (UNI) climbed roughly 25% over the same window, with a broad DeFi index up 16%. It's tempting to point at one headline and call it the cause. The more honest answer is that at least four separate stories landed in the same two-week stretch, and they don't all deserve equal credit.

Catalyst one: the Fed, and then the relief after it

A Federal Reserve rate decision earlier in the week came in as a hike rather than the cut some traders had priced in, which rattled risk assets broadly, crypto included. The rally followed once that initial anxiety faded — helped by the 10-year Treasury yield slipping back under 5% and oil prices easing, both of which took some pressure off the inflation narrative that had spooked markets in the first place. This is a real catalyst, but it's a macro one — it explains why risk appetite returned broadly, not specifically why crypto, and DeFi in particular, outperformed other risk assets.

Catalyst two: the SEC's tokenized-stock exemption

The SEC's new framework letting tokenized U.S. stocks trade on decentralized exchanges names AMMs like Uniswap, Aerodrome, and Raydium directly as the kind of venue this relief was built for. That's about as direct a fundamental catalyst as a DeFi token can get — actual new, regulator-sanctioned volume potentially routing through these specific protocols, not just sentiment. This is the catalyst most likely to be load-bearing for the DeFi-specific portion of the rally, distinct from the broader market recovery.

Catalyst three: the CFTC's parallel rulemaking push

The CFTC sending its own market-structure proposal to the White House the same week reinforces the same theme: two regulators moving on crypto market structure without waiting on Congress. On its own, a proposal sent for interagency review doesn't create new trading volume — but it adds to a sentiment stack that's been building since the SEC's exemption, and markets often price coordinated regulatory momentum ahead of the specifics actually being finalized.

Coverage of the rally has pointed to “mounting market optimism surrounding friendly, coordinated crypto regulations from the SEC and CFTC” as a meaningful piece of the recovery, alongside the macro relief.

Which of these are actually real, and which are sentiment

The Fed relief and the SEC's exemption are the two catalysts with a direct, traceable mechanism — one eases the macro backdrop for all risk assets, the other creates actual new regulator-sanctioned use cases for specific DeFi protocols. The CFTC news and the general "regulatory momentum" framing are real, but softer — they're sentiment amplifiers riding on top of the first two, not independent drivers with their own transaction volume behind them. That distinction matters for anyone deciding whether this rally reflects a durable shift or a short-term repricing that unwinds once the macro backdrop changes again.

What to watch next

The cleanest signal going forward is whether AMM trading volume in tokenized equities actually materializes under the SEC's new exemption over the coming weeks — that's the one catalyst here with a concrete, checkable outcome. If it does, this rally has real fundamentals behind at least part of it. If DeFi volume stays roughly flat while token prices retrace, that's a strong signal this month was mostly sentiment catching up to a friendlier macro backdrop, dressed up in regulatory optimism.
