Staking Finally Showed Up Inside a U.S. Crypto ETF

Grayscale's Ethereum ETF became the first U.S.-listed spot crypto fund to pay out staking rewards. Canary Capital wants to take it further, with a staked SEI ETF designed to keep 90% of assets actively staked.

By ChainBrief Staff ·  September 21, 2026 ·  6 min read
Share on X Share on LinkedIn

For as long as U.S. spot crypto ETFs have existed, they've had a structural gap compared to actually holding the underlying asset yourself: an investor holding Ethereum directly can stake it and earn a yield for helping secure the network, but a fund simply holding ETH in cold storage couldn't pass that yield through to shareholders. That gap started closing when Grayscale enabled staking for its Ethereum products in October 2025, and it became visible in dollar terms in January 2026, when the Grayscale Ethereum Staking ETF made its first distribution — $0.083178 per share, covering rewards earned between October and December 2025.

Why this took as long as it did

Staking isn't just "holding" an asset — it means locking it into a network's validation process, which introduces operational questions a passive custody arrangement doesn't have: which validator does the fund's ETH get staked through, what happens if that validator is penalized ("slashed") for misbehavior, and how does the fund handle the unstaking delay if it needs to meet redemptions. Those are legitimate structural questions, not just regulatory foot-dragging, and they're the reason staking inside a U.S.-listed ETF wrapper took meaningfully longer to arrive than the ETFs themselves.

“Distributing staking rewards to ETHE shareholders is a landmark moment, not just for Grayscale, but for the entire Ethereum community,” Grayscale CEO Peter Mintzberg said.

Who else has followed

Since Grayscale's first distribution, other issuers — including products from Rex Shares/Osprey Funds and 21Shares — have launched their own staking-enabled Ethereum ETFs. Canary Capital has taken the idea further with a proposed staked SEI ETF, currently in amended S-1 filing stage rather than trading, designed to keep around 90% of fund assets actively staked rather than sitting idle — a meaningfully more aggressive staking ratio than a fund that merely offers staking as an option. SEI jumped as much as 16.5% on news of the filing, though it's worth being precise about what that reaction is actually pricing: anticipation of a product that hasn't launched yet, not a live fund.

The tradeoff investors are actually buying

A staking ETF isn't simply "the same fund, plus free yield." Staked assets are less liquid on a short timeline — unstaking isn't instant on most networks — which can affect how quickly a fund can meet redemptions during a period of heavy selling. It's the standard yield-versus-liquidity tradeoff that shows up throughout fixed income and cash management, just newly wrapped in a crypto ETF structure. None of that makes the yield fake; it does mean the product is a genuinely different risk profile than a plain spot ETF, not just the same fund with a bonus attached.

Why this fits the broader pattern

This is part of the same theme running through most of this week's crypto news: regulated products quietly absorbing features that used to live only in crypto-native tools. Staking has been available to anyone running their own validator or using a centralized exchange's staking service for years. What changed is that it now exists inside a wrapper institutional and retail investors already trust and already hold in ordinary brokerage accounts — the same structural shift we've been tracking across stablecoins, tokenized equities, and now yield-bearing crypto funds.
