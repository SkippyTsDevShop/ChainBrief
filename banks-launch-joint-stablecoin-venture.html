Twenty-One of the World's Biggest Banks Are Building Their Own Stablecoin

Bank of America, Citi, Goldman Sachs, Deutsche Bank, UBS, and at least sixteen more institutions are forming a company to issue a dollar stablecoin by 2027 — a direct answer to Circle and Tether from the banks stablecoins were originally built to route around.

By ChainBrief Staff ·  September 21, 2026 ·  6 min read
Share on X Share on LinkedIn

Twenty-one banks and financial institutions have committed to forming a joint venture that will issue a U.S. dollar stablecoin, with the company itself targeted for incorporation in the second half of 2026 and a market launch in the first half of 2027. Among the institutions named so far: Bank of America, Citi, Goldman Sachs, Deutsche Bank, UBS, Santander, Wells Fargo, MUFG Bank, Fidelity Investments, and Standard Bank. The remaining participants haven't been publicly identified.

What we actually know

Not much, yet, beyond the timeline and the bank list. The announcement didn't name the venture itself, its ownership structure, its executives, or which blockchain it plans to build on — only that products will be "available on public blockchains." That's an unusual amount of ambiguity for a group this large to announce publicly, and it's worth reading as a group staking a claim to the idea before the operational details are settled, rather than a fully formed product launch.

Why now

The consortium says it intends to comply with the U.S. GENIUS Act — the federal stablecoin framework that set reserve and licensing requirements for dollar-pegged tokens — and the European Union's Markets in Crypto-Assets (MiCA) regulation, where applicable. That's the same regulatory backdrop we described in an earlier piece on stablecoins becoming financial infrastructure rather than just a crypto-trading convenience. The difference this time is who's building the rail. Non-bank issuers like Circle and Tether built the first generation of dollar stablecoins largely outside the traditional banking system. A 21-bank consortium is the incumbents deciding to own that infrastructure themselves rather than keep watching non-banks capture the fee-generating layer of payments they used to control.

The Circle parallel

This lands roughly a week after Circle launched Arc, its own permissioned layer-1 blockchain built around USDC, with a validator set that includes BlackRock, DTCC, Mastercard, and Visa. Different companies, same underlying instinct: institutions that need settlement certainty are increasingly choosing to build or co-own the infrastructure rather than plug into an existing crypto-native rail they don't control. A bank-owned stablecoin and a bank-adjacent blockchain are two different bets on the same premise — that the next phase of stablecoin adoption runs through institutions that already hold regulatory licenses, not around them.

The euro token and beyond

The consortium has said a euro-denominated token is the next priority after the dollar product, with eventual ambitions toward other Group of Seven currencies. That's a meaningfully larger ambition than a single U.S. dollar stablecoin — it reads as banks trying to build multi-currency settlement infrastructure for themselves, not just compete with USDC on one axis.

What to watch

The real test isn't the announcement — it's whether this stablecoin actually displaces volume from USDC and USDT, or simply adds another option that mostly gets used for bank-to-bank settlement rather than the broader payments and remittance use cases where existing stablecoins have real traction. We'll also be watching for the entity's name, its governance structure, and which blockchain it chooses — all still undisclosed as of this writing.
