Wall Street's Biggest Futures Exchange Now Trades Crypto Like Crypto

CME Group's regulated bitcoin and ether futures never used to close for the weekend — until they didn't have to. Now the exchange that helped legitimize crypto derivatives has gone fully 24/7.

By ChainBrief Staff ·  September 27, 2026 ·  6 min read
Share on X Share on LinkedIn

Since May 29, CME Group's cryptocurrency futures and options have traded continuously — 24 hours a day, seven days a week, with only a two-hour weekly maintenance window. It's a small operational detail with an ironic backstory: for years, one of the most-cited critiques of crypto markets was that they never close, making them harder to regulate and easier to manipulate overnight. Now the largest regulated derivatives exchange in the world has decided that's actually the format worth copying.

The gap this closes

Crypto-native exchanges have run 24/7/365 since the beginning — there's no bell to open or close a market for an asset that trades globally with no single home exchange. CME's futures and options, by contrast, ran on a schedule closer to traditional derivatives: open through the week, closed for the weekend. That gap meant a trader hedging bitcoin exposure through CME carried unhedged weekend risk that a trader using a crypto-native perpetual-futures venue simply didn't. Trading now begins each week Friday at 4:00 p.m. Central on CME Globex, and weekend or holiday trades are recorded under the following business day's trade date for clearing and settlement purposes — a bookkeeping detail that still preserves standard settlement mechanics even though the market itself never technically shuts.

The numbers behind the decision

Tim McCourt, CME Group's Global Head of Equities, FX and Alternative Products, tied the move directly to demand: "Client demand for risk management in the digital asset market is at an all-time high, driving a record $3 trillion in notional volume across our Cryptocurrency futures and options in 2025." That's the kind of volume that turns a scheduling gap from a minor inconvenience into a competitive liability — institutional desks running real risk books don't want to be unhedged for 60-plus hours every week just because the exchange they're required to use for regulatory or compliance reasons happens to be closed.

Traditional finance is catching up to crypto, not the other way around

This isn't happening in isolation. Nasdaq has confirmed a nearly 23-hour trading day for U.S. equities launching in December, leaving stocks closed for just one hour out of every 24. Between that and CME's move, 2026 is turning into the year traditional market structure quietly adopts the always-on assumption crypto markets have operated under from day one — a rare instance of crypto's own design constraints becoming the model other asset classes converge toward, rather than the other way around.

Why it matters if you don't trade CME futures

Even a reader who has never opened a futures account is exposed to this through the products built on top of it. CME's bitcoin and ether futures are the reference price a lot of spot ETFs, options products, and institutional risk models lean on — a market that can now absorb a weekend news shock in real time, rather than gapping open Monday morning after two and a half days of pent-up crypto volatility, changes how those downstream products behave around big weekend headlines. It's also a small but real data point on institutional seriousness: exchanges don't rebuild clearing and risk infrastructure for a niche product. CME made this investment because enough of its clients were already treating crypto futures as core, not speculative, business. Expect the practical effects to show up gradually rather than all at once: fewer of the outsized Sunday-night gap moves that have historically rattled crypto markets before Monday's traditional open, tighter arbitrage between spot and futures prices around weekend events, and, over time, pressure on other regulated exchanges to match CME's hours or risk losing order flow to a venue that never closes. There's a second-order effect worth watching too: as the reference futures market itself trades continuously, the products built on top of it — ETF net-asset-value calculations, options pricing models, risk desks' overnight hedging — inherit that same continuity, which quietly removes one more excuse for treating crypto market infrastructure as less mature than equities or commodities. For a sector that has spent years hearing that it lacks the plumbing of traditional finance, a regulated exchange rebuilding its own clearing systems around crypto's hours, rather than the other way around, is a quieter but arguably more durable form of validation than any single price rally.

Frequently asked

Does this mean I can trade CME bitcoin futures on my phone at 3 a.m. on a Saturday?

If your broker offers access to CME Globex during the new hours, yes — the market itself is now open continuously aside from a roughly two-hour weekly maintenance window. Actual access still depends on your specific broker's own hours and platform.

Is this the same as a crypto exchange's perpetual futures?

No. CME's contracts are standard, expiring futures and options traded on a regulated, centrally cleared exchange — different from the perpetual (never-expiring) futures crypto-native exchanges popularized, though both now share the always-on trading schedule.

Why did CME wait this long to go 24/7?

Regulated derivatives exchanges built their infrastructure, clearing, and risk systems around scheduled market hours; extending to continuous trading required real operational and risk-management changes, not just a policy decision — CME cited record 2025 volume as the trigger to finally make the investment.
